# Decisions: Audio Capture Hotkeys

Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md on 2026-09-29. Add new decisions as a dated entry at the bottom. 

- `2026-07-23` — Warm mic is always-on: the settings toggle was removed and `Config.load()` migrates `warm_mic` back to True (it is how the first word survives stream-open latency). Pre-roll seed raised 0.35s→0.6s to cover hotkey-dispatch latency plus speech starting on the press. `auto_paragraphs` gained a settings toggle (default on). `auto_punctuate` toggle deliberately kept for now — remove only once auto-punctuation is proven correct. Fix All / Email prompts rewritten (copy-editor pass that keeps every word bar stutter duplicates; email keeps dictated sign-offs, never invents greetings or pleasantries) and validated through the real `AIRefiner` path on `google/gemini-2.5-flash-lite`.

- `2026-07-28` — Evidence-based mic auto-selection. Rejected always-on multi-mic capture (lights every mic's in-use indicator, forces Bluetooth headsets into low-quality HFP even when idle, fights the PortAudio lifecycle invariants). Instead: when a recording runs 1.6s voice-free in auto mode, a one-shot ~0.9s level-only probe samples up to 4 other wired mics (rank ≤ 2 — never BT hands-free or loopback, incl. localised loopback names). Verdict needs SUSTAINED energy (≥0.3s above the floor — one transient never counts) beating the primary by 4× RMS. The probe only ARMS advice (stamped with the recording it observed); demotion + evidence preference are committed exclusively in `consume_mic_advice()` after a confirmed no-speech result — a successful dictation must never lose its mic to the next watchdog refresh, and a stale stamp from a superseded recording is discarded. The no-speech path waits ≤1s for an in-flight verdict, switches the warm stream on a background thread, and tells the user to dictate again. Evidence resets when a NEW device appears or the preferred one vanishes (a device merely disappearing must not cancel a just-announced switch) or on manual selection. Watchdog and `_refresh_portaudio` gained a `_probe_active` guard (PortAudio must never re-init with probe streams open; the flag rolls back if the probe thread fails to spawn). Guarded by `tests/test_mic_selection.py`, `tests/test_inject_failure_detail.py`, `tests/test_error_events.py`.

- `2026-08-07` — The start/stop cue is Glaido's, measured not guessed (v1.6.51). Four attempts had missed (too high, too bright, then `MessageBeep` — which is the Windows ERROR chime, the sound users silence system-wide). Glaido ships its two cues as 24-bit/48kHz WAVs **embedded in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Glaido\glaido-core.exe`** (RIFF headers at offsets 19033401 and 19056203); they were extracted and analysed. Both are the same shape: two mallet-style **pure sine** taps (2nd harmonic 0.4–1.8%, 3rd 0.2%), ~6ms attack, exponential decay reaching 10% at ~13ms, second tap louder than the first. Rise = 296.6 Hz then 442.4 Hz, 33ms apart, 79ms total; fall = 440.9 Hz then 287.8 Hz, 48ms apart, 94.5ms total. A perfect fifth, D4↔A4, up for start and down for stop. Reproduced (`_tap`/`_two_tap` in `feedback.py`) rather than shipped verbatim — their asset is theirs — at 2× their level, because theirs peaks at -16 dBFS which is thin on laptop speakers. `_SR` went 22050→44100 so the 6ms attack is not smeared. **`transcription_complete` is now silent**: two cues per dictation, not three — the falling cue already means "got it", and with sub-second stop latency a third tap landed under half a second later and read as clutter. Guarded by `tests/test_feedback_sounds.py`, which pins the pitches, the order (start rises, stop falls), the gap between taps and the absence of MessageBeep.

- `2026-08-07` — The keyboard-hook hotkey fallback actually suppresses the base key now (v1.6.51). When Win32 `RegisterHotKey` loses the combo to another app, registration falls back to the `keyboard` library — and it called `_install_base_key_suppressor()`, which bails unless `_win32_ok`, **which is False by definition on that path**. So the suppressor was never installed and every press sent Alt+V straight to the focused app (a menu accelerator in most), auto-repeating in hold mode. It cannot be fixed by relaxing that guard: the keyboard library runs blocking hooks first and returns the moment one suppresses, BEFORE `queue.put`, so a separate non-blocking detector would stop firing on any press we swallowed. `_install_fallback_combo_hooks()` therefore registers ONE blocking press hook that does both — and gates on `_combo_modifiers_held()` so typing a bare "v" is never touched, dispatches detection to a thread (a blocking hook runs inline in the global input path), and fails OPEN on any exception. Guarded by `tests/test_hotkey_fallback.py`.

- `2026-08-07` — The auto-stop safety timer names the owning bind (v1.6.51). `_auto_stop_recording` called `_on_key_down()`/`_on_key_up()` with the default `source="main"`, and both handlers ignore a source that does not own the recording — so with `max_recording_duration`/`toggle_timeout` set, a recording started by the PTT bind could never be auto-stopped. That timer is the only thing that recovers a recording whose key-up was never seen (stuck key, driver hiccup); without it the app sits on "Recording" and ignores the hotkey until restarted, which is the exact failure the cap exists to catch.

- `2026-08-10` — "Change Shortcut" now records the keys you actually press (v1.6.53). Two stacked defects, both measured live. **(1) The global binds stayed registered during capture.** `RegisterHotKey` swallows its combo before any window sees it, so pressing a CURRENT shortcut delivered nothing to the recorder — and fired the action instead. The refine trigger copies the selection with a synthetic Ctrl+C, so pressing ALT+R recorded **CTRL+C** (reproduced against the running app: the capture window saw VK 0xFF from `_mask_menu_tap`, then the injected Ctrl+C). Fix: `HotkeyManager.suspend()/resume()` and `TriggerHotkeyManager.suspend()/resume()` genuinely unregister — a flag that merely ignores the callback still leaves the keystroke invisible. `AppWindow` suspends on every recorder start and resumes on every stop, plus `_cancel_hotkey_capture()` on leaving the Hotkey tab and on `_hide()`, because a capture stranded mid-flight leaves the app with NO hotkeys until restart. `update_hotkey()` clears `_suspended` itself: the save lands before the recorder's resume. **(2) The combo was read off tkinter's keysym + `event.state`.** Measured: `Alt+;` arrives as keysym `semicolon`, which `_vk_code()` cannot resolve, so that shortcut saved and never registered; the same key reports `R` under Alt but `r` under Shift; and Windows folds Ctrl+Alt into AltGr so Tk reports **only its Ctrl bit** (Ctrl+Alt+R captured as CTRL+R). The base key now comes from `event.keycode` via `_VK_KEY_NAMES` (the exact inverse of `hotkey_manager._VK_MAP` — a test pins the round-trip, since an unresolvable name means vk 0 and a bind that never registers), and modifiers are the UNION of `event.state` and `GetAsyncKeyState` (a modifier can be missed, never invented). Also: all three binds now refuse each other's combo and refuse a bare character key (it would fire while you type — F-keys and the other no-character keys are still allowed), and a save reports what actually registered, so a combo another app owns says so instead of showing "Shortcut updated" over a dead key. NB `Win+key`, `Alt+Space` and Caps Lock deliver no Tk KeyPress at all and therefore cannot be captured (pre-existing); Ctrl+Alt cannot be verified by synthetic input because SendInput refuses to hold both. Guarded by `tests/test_hotkey_capture.py`. **`resume()` restores the state `suspend()` found, it never invents one** (v1.6.54) — the dashboard is reachable before `_init_core` registers anything, so a capture opened and closed in those first seconds would otherwise register the binds early and fire a recording into a pipeline that does not exist yet.

- `2026-08-19` — The start cue fires at the PRESS, not after the mic verify loop (v1.6.71). **The sound itself is unchanged and must stay that way** (see the invariant below). `_on_start_recording` runs on a spawned thread and only reaches `feedback.recording_started()` after `recorder.start()` has waited for the next audio callback — measured 30-95 ms on a live warm stream, with a 0.45s `_START_VERIFY_SECONDS` ceiling on a bad one. The cue now fires at the top of `_on_state_change`, which runs synchronously on the hotkey thread microseconds after the key goes down. Gated on the new `Recorder.warm_capture_live` (warm enabled AND `_stream_looks_alive()`): when that is true the mic is already recording the room and the pre-roll ring holds the moment of the press, so "go" is honest at the instant it plays. When it is false the press has to open or recover a stream and the cue stays where it was, after audio is proven flowing — cueing someone to speak into a stream that is not up yet is precisely how first words get lost. `_start_cue_played` makes it exactly once per press, and is set before the thread is spawned so there is no race. `warm_capture_live` fails CLOSED on any error. Guarded by `tests/test_recorder_instant_start.py`.

- `2026-10-05` - A hung Windows audio engine gets a one-click restart. `audiodg.exe` hung and Windows Audio was killed for hanging, while the hung `audiodg.exe` lived on: every app's mic stream carried nothing, and Echo's dead-capture path said "reconnected" five times in a row because reopening our own stream cannot help. Now a second dead capture within 5 minutes, or one while `Audiosrv` is not running (`sc query`, no admin), shows a toast with a **Restart audio** button; the tray menu has **Restart Windows audio** at any time. Both run `audio_engine.restart()`: one UAC prompt, then an elevated script kills `audiodg.exe`, kills the Audiosrv host process only when it hosts Audiosrv alone (`Restart-Service` can wait forever on a hung service), and starts the service; then the warm stream reopens. It cannot be silent: a per-user app has no admin rights. Guarded by `tests/test_audio_engine_restart.py`.
