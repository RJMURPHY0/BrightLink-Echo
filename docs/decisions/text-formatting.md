# Decisions: Text Formatting

Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md on 2026-09-29. Add new decisions as a dated entry at the bottom. 

- `2026-07-23` — Pause-punctuation hardening. Paragraph breaks now need the pause AND the text to agree (previous part ends `.!?` AND next starts a capital; `PARAGRAPH_PAUSE` 2.0→2.5s) — the old "supply a period at an unpunctuated break" branch is what shipped `",."` artefacts and mid-sentence paragraphs on thinking pauses. At assembly, a chunk-final period contradicted by a lowercase continuation is dropped (batch twin of the live `_pending_punct` rule). `polish()` and the whisper cleanup replace a trailing `,;:` instead of stacking a period. `context_fix` scope widened to pause punctuation/capitalisation artefacts (word-count and substitution guards unchanged — it still never rewords). Join logic guarded by `tests/test_stream_assembly.py`.

- `2026-08-17` — Pause-punctuation: the number and lone-adverb cases (v1.6.69). Reported with a real example, "my Claude OS is set up 100%. Correctly." Two gaps: (1) `_STOP_BEFORE_LOWER` required `[a-z]{2,}` before the stop, so a stop after a NUMBER or percentage could never match it — `100%.` was invisible; (2) nothing handled a stop followed by a CAPITALISED word, because in general that IS a real boundary. The one shape that never is: a "sentence" consisting of a single `-ly` adverb and nothing else. `_LONE_ADVERB_SENTENCE` merges only that, and only when the adverb is the WHOLE sentence — "Obviously, we should ask" and "Basically the whole thing" are normal openers and are left alone. **Rule order is load-bearing**: the lone-adverb rule runs FIRST, because `_CONJ_AFTER_STOP` rewrites ". And" → ", and" and would replace the adverb's own terminator with a comma, leaving "100%. Correctly, and then…" unfixable (caught in test). Validated against the 200 real transcripts in `history.json`: the rules change **exactly one**, which is the reported example, and the other 32 ". Capital" adverb boundaries (". Also", ". Just", ". Obviously") are correctly untouched. Guarded by `tests/test_lone_adverb_punctuation.py`, including a corpus regression test that fails if the rules ever rewrite real dictation into a shape none of the three rules explain. **Known limit, stated rather than papered over:** genuinely ambiguous boundaries need an LLM reading context, and `context_fix` is a BACKGROUND pass that runs AFTER injection (`app.py:1839`) — its result is offered in the popup, not applied to the document. So the injected text is only ever as good as the deterministic rules; putting an LLM on the injection path would cost 1-2s of stop latency, which the product's core promise forbids.

- `2026-08-17` — "Fix All" is a Grammarly-class pass now (v1.6.69). `REFINE_PROMPTS["punctuation"]` (the ✨ Fix All button, `popup.py:1394`) was a general "proofread like a copy editor" instruction; it is now an explicit checklist — spelling, confused homophones (their/there, its/it's, affect/effect), possessive and contraction apostrophes, sentence boundaries left by dictation pauses, capitalisation, subject-verb agreement, tense consistency, plurals, articles and prepositions, stutter duplicates. The explicit list beats a vague instruction: the model reliably fixes what it is named and skips what it is not. The correction-not-rewrite limits are unchanged and were tightened in wording (no restyling, no reordering, no new content, British spelling preserved, only stutter duplicates may be deleted).

- `2026-09-09` — **Spoken addresses, percentages and quotes (v1.6.75).** Reported with two real dictations: "Ryan dot murphy at ftc dash ss dot com" and "of fifty-four percent" came out as prose. "dot", "dash" and "at" are ordinary English words, so they are converted **only inside a run with the SHAPE of an address**, resolved RIGHT-TO-LEFT from a known TLD or file extension: the chain extends left over `.` `-` `_` and, at most once, over `@`; everything further left is prose and is rebuilt exactly as spoken. That right-to-left rule is what makes "email me at ryan dot murphy at example dot com" resolve correctly (first "at" prose, second the `@`) and "I looked at report dot pdf" keep its verb. Two more guards, both from measured failures: everyday-English TLDs (`it`, `in`, `us`, `be`) are **not** in the TLD list, and a one-character leading label is refused outright — without either, "put a dot in the box" became "a.in". Percentages digitise number words only immediately before `percent` (a bare scale word stays prose: "a hundred percent"), and currency words only directly after `$ £ €`. **`quotation mark(s)` is deliberately NOT a rule** — the corpus run caught it turning Ryan's own "research of the other things like quotation marks, dollar signs" into a stray quote; the unambiguous `open quote` / `close quote` / `unquote` forms cover the actual instruction. Validated against the 200 real transcripts in `history.json`: 3 change, all three the wanted rewrite. Guarded by `tests/test_spoken_symbols.py`, which pins both directions plus that corpus run.

- `2026-09-09` — **The full stop at the end of a dictation is now a three-way choice (v1.6.75).** `auto_punctuate` was a boolean that governed the terminal stop AND the pause-artefact repair AND the leading capital, so turning it off to stop the unwanted period silently cost the other two — which is exactly why Ryan reported that "this option has never really worked". New `config.end_punctuation`: `smart` (the new default), `always` (the old behaviour) or `never`, decided in one place, `sentence_end.apply()`, called from BOTH `ParakeetTranscriber.polish()` and `Transcriber._post_process()`. `smart` adds a stop only when the utterance reads finished: not already terminated, not ending on a comma (a comma IS the user saying they have not finished), at least three words, the last word not a hanging function word, and the last token not an address/URL/filename or an open bracket. **The bias is deliberately towards leaving it open** — a missing stop costs one keystroke, an unwanted one has to be hunted down mid-sentence. The `_HANGING` list was **measured, not guessed**: the demonstratives ("I did that", "look at this"), the do-verbs ("all of the phases you need to do", "it's done"), "am" (a clock reading as often as a verb: "till 7 am") and "really" ("not really") were all removed after the corpus showed them blocking genuinely finished sentences. On the 176 real terminated transcripts, smart keeps the stop on 150 and leaves 26 open, and the 26 are fragments or mid-sentence. `auto_punctuate=False` still means "no polish at all" so the existing tests hold; a config with no `end_punctuation` key migrates once to `smart` with `auto_punctuate` forced back on. Settings shows a three-way Dropdown, replacing the toggle. Guarded by `tests/test_sentence_end.py`, which pins both directions and that both engines route through the module.

- `2026-09-09` — **A clearly spoken list comes out as a list (v1.6.76).** Asked for with a live example: "Right, so here's a list of three things. first is like the first thing, second is the second thing" should land as a colon, a blank line and numbered items. New `list_format.py`, applied ONCE on the whole utterance at app.py's post-processing point — **never inside an engine's `_post_process`**, which also runs on streamed chunks and live captions, where a list spanning two commits would be split down the middle and a newline typed into a chat box would send it. Two shapes only: a **numbered list** from ordinal markers at clause boundaries ("first … second", "firstly …", "number one …", with "lastly"/"finally" allowed to CLOSE a run but never start one), and a **bulleted list** from an explicit announcement followed by a short comma series. The guards are what make it safe, because the markers are ordinary English: a run of two needs an ANNOUNCEMENT in the lead-in ("a list of three things"), without one it takes THREE markers, every item needs two words, and a bullet series needs three items of at most eight words each with a real "and"/"or" before the last. Only ONE thing is ever deleted — a bare discourse marker the number now carries ("First, do X" → "1. Do X"); where the marker is part of the sentence ("first is the first thing") it stays, because "1. Is the first thing" is worse than a little redundancy. Text after the list becomes its own paragraph, but only when every other item was a single sentence. Runs BEFORE the user libraries so a snippet BODY is never re-shaped (a body is verbatim, and one holding "First Floor, Second Avenue" is not a list), and is **skipped entirely while Live Typing is on**, where the words are already in the user's document and a restructure would mean deleting and retyping most of them. Validated against the 200 real transcripts in `history.json`: **1 changes, and it is the reported example** — zero false positives. Gated by `config.auto_lists` (default on, Settings toggle). Guarded by `tests/test_list_format.py`, which pins both directions, that no word is ever lost bar a list marker, and the wiring.

- `2026-09-09` — The AI refiner stopped undoing the app's own lists (v1.6.76). `_NO_FORMAT` is appended to every refine prompt and says "no bullet points, numbered lists, dashes" — correct while the app could never PRODUCE one, and actively wrong the moment `list_format` could: the first ✨ Fix All (or the background `context_fix`) would flatten a laid-out list straight back into prose. `refine()` now appends a preserve-this-list clause when `_looks_like_a_list(text)`, and — because a prompt is an instruction, not a guarantee — `context_fix` carries a **structural** guard that rejects any result which lost the list. That one matters most: `context_fix` runs in the background and its result is offered without the user asking, and a flattening keeps every word, so neither the word-count nor the substitution guard would have caught it. Inventing a list is still forbidden.

- `2026-09-14`: **Sentence Endings now governs the stop the ENGINE writes (v1.6.79).** Reported: "Never add a full stop" still ended dictations in one, and Smart still closed mid-sentence dictations. Root cause, measured on the local corpus: Parakeet ends every utterance with a full stop itself, and `sentence_end.apply()` only ever decided whether to ADD a missing one (`already_terminated` returned early). So 172 of the 200 stored dictations ended in a stop under Smart and Never alike, and the v1.6.75 corpus figures ("smart keeps 150, leaves 26 open") described a decision the shipped code never ran. Now `never` removes a sentence-final full stop, and `smart` judges an existing stop with the same `looks_finished()` test it uses to add one. Untouched in both: `?`, `!`, ellipses, and an abbreviation's own dot (`7 p.m.`, `U.S.`, `etc.`). Only the END of the dictation is affected; internal sentence stops stay. On the corpus Smart removes 25 of 172 stops: 21 are fragments, search-box entries or addresses (`at the.`, `but.`, `Trapezium shape.`, `ryan.murphy@ftc-ss.com.`), 4 are finished sentences ending on a `_HANGING` word (`if you can.`, `talking about.`), which is the stated lean towards leaving it open. The AI `context_fix` upgrade gets the inserted text's ending via `sentence_end.match_ending()` at all three call sites in app.py, otherwise accepting an upgrade put the stop back (a test pins that every `context_fix(` call is wrapped). Known limit, stated rather than hidden: Smart reads only the words, so a grammatically complete clause dictated into the middle of a sentence still keeps its stop. Guarded by `tests/test_sentence_end.py`, including a corpus invariant that each mode only ever settles the final stop.

- `2026-09-21`: **Dictation into an email client is laid out as an email (v1.6.81).** Asked for directly: in Outlook or Gmail, "Hi John, thanks for your email … Kind regards, Ryan" should land with the greeting, the sign-off and the name on their own lines, and nothing anywhere else should change. New `email_format.py`, applied once at app.py's post-processing point after `format_lists` and before the user libraries (a snippet body, which may BE a signature, stays verbatim), skipped under Live Typing, gated by `config.email_format` (default on, Settings toggle "Format Emails"). **The gate is the app, read at record start**: `is_email_app()` accepts Outlook (classic `outlook`, new `olk`), Windows Mail (`hxoutlook`) and Thunderbird by exe, and a BROWSER only when its title has Gmail or Outlook as a separator-delimited segment ("… - Gmail", "Mail - Ryan Murphy - Outlook"), and only when nothing but an Edge profile name sits between that segment and the browser's own name. So a Google, Bing or DuckDuckGo results page never counts, even for a query that itself reads "sign in - Gmail", and neither does "Create filters - Gmail Help", "How to fix Outlook - Google Search" or a Word doc titled "… - Outlook" (Ryan's explicit requirement: search must never get the layout). The title is read with the app identity on the hotkey thread, because a browser title follows the active tab. **Only shape changes**: line breaks, the punctuation beside them, and the capital that now starts the body; every word stays in order (pinned). A greeting needs a greeting word plus either punctuation or a capitalised addressee, so "Morning meeting moved" is left alone, and a capitalised sentence starter is trimmed off the addressee ("Hi John I wanted" keeps "I" in the body). "Thanks John" is the one real ambiguity (sender signing off or recipient being thanked): a thanks-type phrase takes the name as a signature only when it matches the signed-in user's first name (`_sender_name`, from the same profile fetch as the Email refine), or, with no name known, when it is not the greeting's addressee; a whole reply of "Thanks." is never touched. Just a greeting ("Hi John") ends in a blank line so the next press lands on the body line, and Add Trailing Space no longer appends after a trailing newline. `context_fix` now rejects any result that changes the line count (the email layout and auto paragraphs are shape the LLM must not undo; same words, so neither existing guard would catch it) and is told so in its prompt; the whisper upgrade result gets the same layout. On the 200 real transcripts, treated as if every one went into an email, 1 changes ("Hello, I want." gets its own greeting line). Guarded by `tests/test_email_format.py` (32 tests, both directions, wiring, app detection, refiner guard, corpus).

- `2026-09-21`: **"Number one … number two" and "bullet point one" come out as lists (v1.6.84).** Reported against a competitor's output: "So, I want to start a list for a few things that I want to do, so, number one, … Number two, …" stayed as prose. Two misses in `list_format.py`: `_ANNOUNCE` wanted "a few OF things" so "a few things" / "start a list" never counted as an announcement, and the ", so," in front of "number one" broke the marker match (a connective could not carry its own comma). Now: connectives may carry a comma and are dropped with the marker, `_ANNOUNCE` knows "start/make a list", "list for" and "a few/couple/handful of things"; `number N` also takes digits (`number 1`); **spoken bullets** are a new marker kind ("bullet point one", "bullet 2", "bullet point number three", "next bullet point", bare "bullet point") and produce `•` lines. "Number N" and bullet markers are EXPLICIT, so two of them make a list without an announcement and are always removed from the item ("1. Number one, I want" would be silly); ordinals keep the old rules (announcement or three markers, kept when part of the sentence). Bullet and number markers never mix in one run. An explicit run gives its lead-in the colon unless the lead-in is a finished sentence. Still one change over the 200 real transcripts, and it is the reported example; "my number one priority" and "we scored number one" stay prose. Guarded by `tests/test_list_format.py`.

- `2026-09-21`: **Spoken lists, second pass: no comma needed, "the first bullet point is", full stops and dash bullets (v1.6.85).** v1.6.84 still failed two live dictations compared side by side with a competitor. (1) "…one thing **and number two** i want…": markers were only recognised straight after punctuation, and people rarely pause long enough for the engine to write a comma before "and number two", so only one marker was found. The separator may now be bare whitespace, but ONLY in front of an explicit marker (number N, bullet forms, "the second thing is") and ONLY when the lead-in announced a list; ordinals and "finally" still need punctuation. That is what keeps "we scored number one in the league and number two in the cup" and "I came first and second" as prose. (2) "…so **the first bullet point is** CPU energy and **the second bullet point is** GPU energy": the phrasing was unknown. New forms: "(the) first/second/…/next/last bullet (point) (is)" → bullets; "(the) first/second/… thing/point/item/one/step/reason **is**" → numbers, where the verb is REQUIRED so "the first thing I noticed was…" stays prose. Also: "to list" / "let me list" count as announcements; "for" and "like" are connectives ("you know, for number two"); the same slot said twice in a row ("first, so like number one, …") counts once, with the later marker's body. **Output changes:** bullets are `- ` not `•` (plain text everywhere, a real bullet in every Markdown app; the competitor does the same), and items that are sentences end in a full stop while labels ("CPU energy") do not, decided by majority for the whole list (`_is_sentence`: a subject pronoun or a common verb, 3+ words). A lower-case lone "i" inside an item is capitalised. On the 200 real transcripts exactly 5 change, all of them Ryan's list test dictations, all correct. Guarded by `tests/test_list_format.py` (42).

- `2026-09-22`: **The BrightLink inbox counts as an email client, and a "kind regards" close takes its own lines everywhere (v1.6.86).** Reported with a screenshot of the CRM's New message composer: "Hi John, thanks for getting back to me so soon and I just wanted to say thank you kind regards Ryan" landed as one line. Two causes. (1) The inbox is a panel over whatever page is open, not a route, so the tab kept reading "Home | BrightLink" and `is_email_app` had nothing to match. The CRM now names the tab itself (Brightlink `src/lib/pageTitle.ts`, `INBOX_TITLE` / `NEW_EMAIL_TITLE`, written with `usePageTitle` by `Inbox.tsx` while open and by `UnifiedSendModal.tsx` on its Email tab, pinned by the CRM's `tests/page-title.test.tsx`), and `_is_crm_mail_title` accepts a browser title that is exactly "Inbox | <brand>" or "New email | <brand>", with at most an Edge profile after the brand and no search engine. Only the page half can be matched because the brand half is the customer's profile name. Renaming either string on the CRM side silently turns this off, which is why both sides pin it. (2) The sign-off needed punctuation in front of it, and with no pause before "kind regards" the engine wrote none. A sign-off may now run straight on from the body, but only a phrase in `_RUN_ON` (the adjective + "regards" closes, "yours sincerely/faithfully") or a casual close ("cheers", "thanks") followed by the signed-in user's own first name, and never when the word before it makes it the object of the sentence (`_GOVERNS`: "give him my kind regards", "sign it off kind regards Ryan", "sent with kind regards"). "With kind regards", "all the best", "best wishes" and "sincerely" still need a pause, because "end the email with kind regards Ryan" and "I wish you all the best Ryan" are sentences. New `format_signoff()` runs in EVERY other app (Ryan's call: nobody says "kind regards" except to close a message): only an `_ANYWHERE` close (the "regards" family and the formal ones) WITH a name after it moves, onto a blank line then "<Close>," and "<Name>" on the line below; the greeting and body stay exactly as spoken. Same `email_format` setting and Live Typing exemption, applied at the same point and to the whisper upgrade result. On the 200 real transcripts exactly one changes in each mode, the reported example. Guarded by `tests/test_email_format.py` (both directions, the any-app rule over the corpus may only ever produce a named close).

- `2026-09-25`: **A refine keeps the exact spacing the text had.** Reported with an Outlook email: three blank lines before the last paragraph came back as one. The model normalises whitespace whatever the prompt says, so `ai_refiner.restore_spacing()` re-imposes it on every refine result (all modes). Same number of content lines: every gap, indent and leading/trailing edge is the original's, each line's words are the model's. Lines merged or split but the same paragraph count: the paragraph gaps and edges are the original's. Anything else is a real restructure and is left alone; single-line text is never touched. Separators are copied verbatim, so a CRLF selection stays CRLF. Skipped when a custom Ask talks about layout (`_LAYOUT_ASK`: lines, paragraphs, spacing, blank, format, list…), since restoring would undo the ask. Only whitespace ever changes. Guarded by `tests/test_refine_spacing.py`.

- `2026-09-25`: **Spoken lists stop firing on ordinary speech, and refine keeps what was said (v1.6.99).** Reported with a screenshot: "…if I could get the usage for the subscriptions I'm genuinely paying for, that are, you know, like AI related, and still good" came out as five bullets ("That are", "You know"). Cause: `_ANNOUNCE` matched "the usage **there is** actually being used" 17 words before the series, and nothing checked the items. The corpus held three more of the same (2026-09-21, 09-23, 09-24), all Ryan talking, all now literal fixtures in `tests/test_list_format.py::NaturalSpeechTests`. Four guards in `list_format.py`, each alone blocking its case and each pinned by its own test (mutation-checked): an announcement counts only within `_ANNOUNCE_REACH` (8) words of the items (`_announced`, used by both shapes); a comma series after a lead-in that stops on a `_HANGING` function word at a full stop ("get the usage for. The…") is a pause, not a list; every series item must pass `_is_series_item` (no filler items like "you know"/"also"/"everything", no item opening on "that/which/but/so", none ending on a function word); and a marker run whose item reads "sort of thing"/"or something" (`_EXAMPLE_TAIL`) was an example, not a list. All 48 existing and new list tests pass, real lists still form. **Refine:** Fix All got its own `_EDITOR_SYSTEM_PROMPT`: it had been sent the style system prompt ("keep sentences short, avoid filler words"), which contradicted its own "keep every word" rule and made it reword. Fix All now also drops false starts the speaker corrected ("on Tuesday, sorry, Wednesday" -> "on Wednesday") and hesitation sounds (um, uh, er), and, at Ryan's request, filler "like" and "you know" used only as padding (a "like" or "you know" that means something stays: "I like it", "tools like Slack", "you know what I mean?"). Voice phrases like "so yeah" are kept. Formal, casual, concise and email carry `_KEEP_ESSENCE` (wording and tone may change, every point, fact, name, number and the certainty may not; nothing added). An Ask keeps the style prompt and is told to do only what it asks. `_refine_via_openrouter` takes the system prompt from `refine()` so an Ask (sent with the default mode) is never mistaken for Fix All. Guarded by `tests/test_refine_keeps_meaning.py`. Also fixed in this release: the v1.6.98 feedback player's tick raised `KeyError: 'filled'` when the canvas had not been laid out yet (the draw returns early under 60px), so playback never stopped; it now skips the bar update and still ends (`tests/test_history_feedback.py::test_playback_ends_on_its_own`, which had been red since v1.6.98).
